5. Directory and group rosters ​

Step 5 of the setup path, optional. AsyncUp can read your Workspace directory as its service account. That buys three things:

FeatureWhat it needs
Rosters that follow a Google Group: joiners are added, leavers droppedThe admin role below
Google Groups in a space: on a roster picked from the space, members of a group in the space count as in itThe admin role below
User lookups: Workspace admins recognised at Google sign-in, accounts outside the Workspace refused, SAML and SCIM users linked to their Chat identity straight away, emails found for Calendar out-of-officeThe admin role below
Calendar out-of-office: people with an Out of office event are marked awayDomain-wide delegation

No domain-wide delegation is needed for the first two. Google recommends an admin role on the service account instead, and that is what AsyncUp uses.

Give the service account an admin role ​

You need a Workspace super admin for this, once.

  1. In the Google Cloud project that holds the service account, enable the Admin SDK API (APIs & Services → Library).
  2. In the Admin console, go to Account → Admin roles → Create new role. Name it, e.g. AsyncUp Directory Reader.
  3. Under Admin API privileges, tick only Groups › Read and Users › Read. Nothing else.
  4. Open the new role and choose Assign service accounts. Enter the service account's email, e.g. asyncup@<project>.iam.gserviceaccount.com. Assign it to the service account, not to a person: AsyncUp calls Google as the service account.

The built-in Groups Reader role is not enough on its own. It has no Users › Read, which AsyncUp needs to resolve group members and look people up.

A new role can take up to about 15 minutes to apply. Until then:

  • a roster sync says it can't read the group, and names the role to grant;
  • Google sign-in still works, but everyone signs in as a regular user, and the server log says the service account can't read users.

Rosters that follow a Google Group ​

A standup can follow a Google Group instead of people picked from its space. Everyone in the group is on the standup, including members of nested groups.

  • When you create a standup: under People, set Who is on it to Everyone in a Google Group and enter the Google Group email.
  • For an existing standup: Settings → People → Roster, choose Everyone in a Google Group, enter the group's email and save.

The roster syncs straight away, then every hour, again in the hour before each run, and before Run now. Sync now syncs on demand and reports who was added, who was removed, and who was skipped and why: people outside your Workspace, suspended accounts and accounts that aren't active.

How a group roster behaves:

EventResult
Someone joins the groupAdded with the default setup: prompted, required, following the standup's days
Someone leaves the groupDropped from the standup. Their history stays
You untick someone in the People gridThey stay off, even though they're still in the group
You add someone by hand who isn't in the groupThey stay on

Group joiners are not checked for whether the bot can DM them. Make sure the app reaches everyone in the group (step 4).

Rosters picked from the space ​

A roster picked from the space is checked against the space on the same schedule: every hour, and again in the hour before each run.

EventResult
Someone on the roster leaves the spaceTaken off the standup, and out of today's run unless they've answered. Their history stays
They rejoin the spacePut back with their old settings, from the next run
You take someone off by handThey stay off, even if they rejoin
Someone was added from outside the space (API or MCP)Never taken off, because they were never seen in the space
Someone joins the spaceNothing. You pick who is on the standup

When a Google Group is a member of the space, its people count as in the space. AsyncUp reads the group with the admin role above. If it can't, or Google Chat doesn't answer, or the space lists nobody, nobody is taken off and Settings → People → Last sync says why.

The first check after an upgrade only notes who is in the space, so nobody comes off until they leave after that.

User lookups ​

With a service-account key stored, AsyncUp looks people up as the service account:

WhereEffect
Google sign-inAccounts the Directory doesn't know are refused, suspended accounts are refused, and Workspace super or delegated admins get the admin console
SAML sign-inThe person is linked to their Chat identity on first sign-in, and Workspace admins get the admin console. Accounts the Directory doesn't know are still let in: the IdP vouched for them
SCIM provisioningNew users are linked to their Chat identity by email
Calendar out-of-officePeople's emails are found before they have ever used the bot

If the service account can't read users, every one of these falls back to working without the Directory: Google sign-in lets the person in as a regular user, and SAML users are linked the first time they use the bot.

Directory through delegation (legacy) ​

Settings › Google Chat → Directory through delegation (legacy) sets a Workspace admin to act as, which moves Directory lookups from the admin role to domain-wide delegation, impersonating that admin. Leave it off with the admin role in place.

Earlier versions needed delegation for lookups, and installs set up that way keep working. If you deliberately want lookups through delegation, turn it on, enter an admin's email, and delegate https://www.googleapis.com/auth/admin.directory.user.readonly to the service account (see the steps below). Calendar out-of-office never uses this setting or the admin email.

Calendar out-of-office ​

This needs domain-wide delegation, because AsyncUp reads each person's calendar as that person. It does not use the Workspace admin email.

  1. Find the service account's OAuth 2 client ID, a long number. It is shown as Client ID for domain-wide delegation under Settings › Google Chat → Service account, in the guided setup when you switch Calendar out-of-office on, and in the Cloud console on the service account's page.
  2. In the Admin console: Security → Access and data control → API controls → Domain-wide delegation → Add new. Enter the client ID and the scope https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events.readonly.
  3. Enable the Google Calendar API in the Cloud project.
  4. In AsyncUp, switch Calendar out-of-office on: in the guided setup's service-account step, or under Settings › General.

The setting is off in a new install. Turn it on only after delegating: without the delegation, every calendar check fails, nobody is marked away, and each failure is logged.

When a run opens, people with an Out of office event that day are marked away, listed under Away and never counted as missing. AsyncUp needs each person's email to check their calendar: it comes from the Directory (the admin role above), or is learned the first time the person uses the bot.
