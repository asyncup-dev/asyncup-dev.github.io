Roles and permissions ​

Everything in AsyncUp asks for one permission: the web app, the JSON API, Chat commands and MCP tools. A role is a named set of permissions. A person holds a role either across the workspace or on one standup. Their access is everything their roles give them, added together.

Where access comes from ​

SourceWhat the person getsWhere it applies
Google Workspace admin (through the Directory), or the SAML admin groupAdminThe workspace
The operator token (OPERATOR_TOKEN), while token sign-in is onAdminThe workspace
On a standup's rosterMemberThat standup
A standup's admin: ran setup, created it, admin @user, or made a manager on the Team pageManagerThat standup
A grant, by emailThe granted role, built-in or customThe workspace, or one standup

Built-in roles ​

RoleForGranted?
AdminRunning the install: every standup, workspace settings, sign-in, tokens and rolesAcross the workspace only
ManagerRunning standups: settings, people, schedules and blockersAcross the workspace or on one standup
ViewerStakeholders who read but change nothing: answers, blockers, reports, the team, CSV exportAcross the workspace or on one standup
MemberAnswering: everyone on a roster has it on that standupNo, it comes from the roster

Built-in roles can't be changed. Copy one to start a custom role.

schedules.manageChange people's weeks and days off, decide time-off requests✓✓
blockers.manageResolve anyone's blocker✓✓
standups.createCreate standups✓
standups.deleteDelete an archived standup with its history✓
settings.manageWorkspace settings, setup checks, deleting history✓
sso.manageGoogle sign-in, SAML and token sign-in✓
tokens.manageMachine tokens, service MCP tokens, everyone's MCP tokens✓
roles.manageCreate roles, grant them across the workspace, and set the SAML admin group✓

The last six are workspace-only: they mean nothing on a single standup. Some things don't need a permission. People always answer, skip and manage their own days off (within the time-off policy), the owner and tagged people work a blocker, and anonymous moods stay anonymous for every role. Mood never appears in the Chat space, and everyone sees their own mood comment.

Custom roles ​

A custom role is a name, a description and any set of the permissions above. Good uses:

  • Auditor: answers.view, reports.view and export, across the workspace, for someone who reviews but never runs a standup.
  • Coach: schedules.manage and blockers.manage on one standup, for a scrum master who isn't its manager.
  • Help desk: tokens.manage and sso.manage, for IT without the rest of Admin.

Rules ​

RuleWhy
You can only put permissions you hold into a role, and only grant or revoke a role whose permissions you hold at that scopeNobody can hand themselves or others more than they have
Granting across the workspace needs roles.manage. On one standup, people.manage there is enoughManagers can bring in a Viewer for their own standup
A role with a workspace-only permission can only be granted across the workspaceThose permissions have no meaning on one standup
A custom role with any standup permission also gets standups.viewThe others work on a standup the holder can see
You can't revoke or edit away your own roles.manageSomeone else, or the operator token, has to. Admins through Google or SAML are never locked out
A role that anyone holds can't be deletedRevoke its grants first

Grants follow a person's email. Google and SAML sign-ins carry it. In Chat it's the email AsyncUp has learned for that person, from the Directory or their first message to the app. Changes apply on the person's next request.

Managing roles ​

WhereWhat you can do
Web app: Settings › RolesSee every role, its permissions and who holds it. Copy a role, build one from the permission checklist, edit or delete custom roles
Web app: Settings › AccessSee every grant and filter it by role. Grant a role across the workspace or on one standup, revoke, and read the change history
Web app: a standup's Settings › Roles on this standupIts managers grant and revoke roles on that standup
Chat: role @user <role>Grant a role on the space's standup. role @user remove <role> revokes it, and roles lists who holds what there. An email works in place of the mention
JSON API/permissions, /roles, /grants and /roles/audit

Every change to a role or grant is logged with who made it and when.

admin @user in Chat, and Make manager on the Team page, still make someone one of the standup's admins. That gives them Manager there, and status lists them as its admins. Use a grant for access without being named as an admin.

What each person sees ​

  • Web app. Navigation, settings sections, standup tabs and every button follow the person's permissions. A Viewer gets Standups, Blockers, Reports and Team, with no Run now, roster or settings controls. Members get their own page at /app/me.
  • Chat. Commands that change a standup need the permission in the command reference. status, trends, blockers, blocker, polls, export and help are open to everyone in the space.
  • MCP. A personal token acts as its owner with their current roles. It's only offered the tools those roles could use somewhere, and each call is checked again for the standup it names.
  • API. Each route in the OpenAPI document names its permission in x-permission. GET /api/v1/me lists the caller's permissions across the workspace and per standup.

Notes ​

  • A standup always keeps at least one admin; removing the last is refused.
  • A standup created with no admins (for example from a script with the operator token) is open to configuration by anyone in its space. Name an admin when creating on someone's behalf.
  • Who may mark someone away is set per standup by its time-off policy.
  • In the web app, creating a standup goes through the setup walkthrough, so it needs settings.manage as well as standups.create. The API needs only standups.create.
